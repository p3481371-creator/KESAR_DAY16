One flower, three threads

The world's most patient spice.

Saffron is the dried stigma of one flower, Crocus sativus. Nothing about it can be hurried: not the bloom, not the picking, not the sorting. That is why it costs what it does, and why so much of what is sold as saffron is not.

Know the grades

Red is the flavour. Yellow is the filler.

Each thread is a crimson stigma joined to a pale yellow style. The more yellow left on, the weaker the saffron. Pick a grade to see which part of the thread you are paying for.

The water test

Real saffron takes its time.

Drop a thread into a glass of water. Real saffron releases slow golden-yellow streams and the thread itself stays red. Dyed fakes bleed red at once and turn pale.

  • 1Slow, golden colourYellow-gold streams over several minutes, never instant red.
  • 2The thread stays redLift it out: it is still crimson, and soft, not dissolved.
  • 3Trumpet-shaped tipsUnder a light, each real stigma flares at the end like a tiny horn.

Under the loupe

Real threads flare like a trumpet.

Move the glass over the threads. Real Kashmiri saffron has a flared, frilled tip and a deep crimson that fades to orange. Fakes are flat, frayed and the same red all the way.
